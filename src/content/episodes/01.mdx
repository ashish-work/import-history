## The World

Before Python there was ABC — a teaching language built at CWI in Amsterdam, designed to be readable above all else. Indentation instead of braces. High-level types instead of pointer arithmetic. ABC is where Python's DNA comes from, and ABC failed: a closed ecosystem with no extensibility, no way to open a file, no escape hatches to the world outside the language. A beautiful classroom with the doors locked.

Around it, the pressures of late-eighties computing: CWI's Amoeba distributed-OS project needed a scripting language, and the daily reality of Unix work meant choosing between the shell — quick but shapeless — and C — powerful but slow to write. Into that gap, Perl was already moving, on its way to becoming the sysadmin's lingua franca. The Perl rivalry would define Python's first decade.

## The Pressure

The niche was glue. Unix automation, text munging, sysadmin work — jobs that wanted something easier than C and more structured than shell or Perl. Nobody was asking for elegance; they were asking to get things done without segfaults.

The teaching mission never left, either. In 1999 it became explicit, in a DARPA proposal titled "Computer Programming for Everybody" — the idea that programming literacy belonged to everyone, with Python as the vehicle.

## The Response

In February 1991, Guido posted Python 0.9.0 to alt.sources. The name came from Monty Python, not the snake.

In 1992, threads arrived — and with them the Global Interpreter Lock, because Python's reference counting wasn't thread-safe. Understand the moment before judging it: every machine had one core, and clock speeds were doubling every eighteen months. One big lock was simple, correct, and cost nothing you could measure. The GIL was the right call.

Python 1.0 shipped in January 1994. Functional programming arrived by patch, not by design: `lambda`, `map`, `filter`, and `reduce` were contributed, as Guido later put it, "courtesy of (I believe) a Lisp hacker who missed them and submitted working patches." Guido's decade of ambivalence about those features starts here; it pays off later, in the comprehension chapters of this story.

Through the 1.5 era the language grew packages and refined its classes, comp.lang.python built a culture, and a philosophy took shape that would get a name: batteries included. On June 4, 1999, Tim Peters posted "The Python Way" to the list — the aphorisms that would become the Zen of Python, canonized as PEP 20 five years later.

## The Fight

The GIL's first challenger arrived before most people knew the lock existed. In 1996, Greg Stein produced a set of free-threading patches against Python 1.4: the GIL removed, replaced with fine-grained locks. They worked. The cost, in Guido's later recollection: the patches "slowed down single-threaded execution nearly two-fold." And when David Beazley exhumed the patch set in 2011 and actually measured it, he found it four to seven times slower.

Guido's response set the bar that would hold for two decades: no GIL removal that regresses single-threaded performance. Simple to state, brutal to satisfy.

## Why Your Code Looks Like This

Why is indentation syntax? Because ABC did it, and ABC was right about readability even while being wrong about everything else. Why is `self` explicit? Same inheritance. Why does `lambda` feel bolted on? Because it literally was — it arrived in a patch, from someone else, into a language whose designer never fully wanted it.
