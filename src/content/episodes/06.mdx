## The World

Docker in 2013. Kubernetes in 2014. Microservices, SaaS everywhere — and Go 1.0 (2012) starting to eat Python's ops niche while Node's async model owned the "modern server" narrative.

Meanwhile Python codebases crossed a threshold nobody had planned for: a million lines. Dropbox — where Guido himself landed in 2013. Instagram's Django monolith, serving a billion users. OpenStack. Scripts had become systems, and the language was still dressed for scripting.

## The Pressure

Untyped Python at scale means refactoring fear, onboarding cost, and the daily question "what does this function take?" Types arrived as pressure from codebase size, not fashion.

And async needed a standard. The gevent-versus-Twisted-versus-Tornado fragmentation — episode 3's unresolved war — was still splitting the ecosystem three ways.

## The Response

**The typing arc.** Annotations had existed since 3.0 with no semantics at all (PEP 3107) — syntax waiting for a purpose. mypy, Jukka Lehtosalo's redirected thesis project, gave them one. PEP 484 made gradual typing official in 3.5 (2015). Then variable annotations (3.6), dataclasses (3.7, attrs-inspired), Protocols (PEP 544, 3.8). Facebook, Google, and Microsoft each built their own checker — pyre, pytype, pyright — and typeshed became a commons. Types became an industry.

**The async arc.** `yield from` (PEP 380, 3.3), then Guido's own tulip experiment, then asyncio in 3.4, then the real keywords — async/await, PEP 492, in 3.5, driven by Yury Selivanov. Async generators in 3.6, contextvars in 3.7, uvloop underneath. Then the framework story caught fire: ASGI (Andrew Godwin), Starlette and uvicorn in 2018, and FastAPI the same year — async finally got its Rails moment.

**Quality of life.** 3.6 brought f-strings (PEP 498) and ordered dicts — an implementation detail promoted to a guarantee in 3.7. Call it the "Python got nice" release.

**The counterculture.** curio (Beazley, again) and trio (Nathaniel Smith, 2017) argued the stdlib had standardized the wrong design, and the 2018 essay "Go statement considered harmful" made the structured-concurrency case — vindicated years later when TaskGroups and ExceptionGroups landed in 3.11.

## The Fight

The canon of the color war, in order: Glyph's "Unyielding" (2014) making the pro-explicit case; Nystrom's "What Color Is Your Function?" (2015) naming the cost; Ronacher's asyncio-skeptic posts (2016) itemizing the design debt.

The typing backlash ran in parallel: "this isn't Python anymore" versus the people maintaining a million lines. And one fight refused to end: the PEP 563 versus PEP 649 annotations saga starts here and doesn't resolve until 3.14 — thread the needle to episode 8.

## Why Your Code Looks Like This

Why are there two concurrency models in the stdlib? Because episode 3's war ended in a treaty, not a victory. Why does async color your whole call stack? Nystrom named it; the design chose it. Why are type hints optional-but-everywhere? Gradual typing was the only politically possible typing. And why do FastAPI signatures look like that? Because annotations finally earned their keep.
