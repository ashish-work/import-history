## The World

First, the coronation. In November 2015, TensorFlow picked a Python frontend — because episode 4 had already happened: the numpy idioms, the notebooks, the researchers were all in place. PyTorch followed in 2017, ported from Lua Torch, and carried an explicit lesson: Lua had the better runtime story and lost on ecosystem. Keras, then Hugging Face's transformers (2018–19), then ChatGPT in November 2022. The AI boom runs on Python glue over CUDA.

Then, the bill. Cloud economics turned "Python is slow" from a forum argument into a line item. At Instagram and Dropbox scale, interpreter overhead is priced in dollars.

## The Pressure

Two vectors converged on the core at once: AI workloads that want threads — data loaders, serving — hitting the GIL; and general fleet costs demanding a faster interpreter. For the first time in this whole story, the money and the motivation both pointed at the same place.

## The Response

A war on two fronts.

**Front one: make it faster.** Mark Shannon's plan — 5x in four releases — was proposed in October 2020, and Microsoft funded the Faster CPython team in 2021: Guido, Shannon, Brandt Bucher, and colleagues. The results shipped on cadence: 3.11's specializing adaptive interpreter (PEP 659, roughly 25% average) with zero-cost exceptions; 3.12's comprehension inlining (PEP 709) and per-interpreter GIL (PEP 684 — Eric Snow's decade-long subinterpreter arc, finally paying off); 3.13's experimental copy-and-patch JIT (PEP 744); 3.14's tail-call interpreter and stdlib subinterpreters (PEP 734). A sober note belongs here: the <Verify>May 2025 Microsoft layoffs gutted much of the Faster CPython team</Verify> — the speed work continues, but the funding question has reopened.

**Front two: remove the lock.** Recite the lineage: Stein, 1999. The Gilectomy, 2016–17 — Larry Hastings' attempt, dead on the same single-threaded cliff. Then Sam Gross, October 2021: nogil. Biased reference counting, immortal objects, mimalloc, deferred refcounting — the first attempt in twenty-two years that didn't tank single-threaded speed. The community deliberated in the open on discuss.python.org; the Steering Council conditionally accepted PEP 703 in July 2023 with a phased, reversible plan; 3.13 shipped the experimental free-threaded build; and PEP 779 made it officially supported in 3.14 (2025). Meta funded the engineer-years, and the motivation is written right on the tin: PyTorch data loading.

Now say the full-circle beat out loud. The GIL pushed compute into C (episode 1). The C ecosystem won science (episode 4). Science became AI (this episode). And AI's owners paid to remove the GIL.

**Epilogue.** The pressure never stops; it just changes language. The Rust wave is moving in below the line — ruff, uv, polars, pydantic-core, PyO3 — "the new C." CPython landed official iOS and Android tiers in 3.13, and t-strings (PEP 750) in 3.14. Language features are lagging indicators of industry pressure. Roll credits.

## Why Your Code Looks Like This

Why does `python3.13t` exist? Why do some wheels ship two builds? Why do your NumPy and PyTorch upgrade notes suddenly mention free-threading? Because "is the GIL gone?" is now a version question, not a yes/no. After thirty-four years, that is what victory looks like.
